# ENS-WAM (ECMWF Wave Ensemble)

## What this model is
ENS-WAM is the ensemble configuration of **ECWAM**, ECMWF's ocean wave model, providing probabilistic forecasts of sea state out to 15 days. It is the wave counterpart of [IFS ENS](../../../ensemble_models/global/eu/ifs-ens.md) and the ensemble sibling of the deterministic [ECWAM](./ecwam.md) forecast.

ENS-WAM runs the same wave physics on the same grid as the deterministic ECWAM. Its spread is not generated by perturbing the wave model itself — it is inherited almost entirely from the driving atmospheric ensemble, since wave growth is dominated by the surface wind field. Each of the 50 ensemble members is the wave response to one perturbed IFS ENS atmospheric member.

ECMWF distributes ENS-WAM through the free Open Data programme as `stream=waef` at 0.25°, alongside the deterministic wave stream.

> **This entry is filed under `wave_models/` rather than `ensemble_models/`**, following the repository's convention that ensemble marine systems use their phenomenon template. It uses `wave-model.template.md` with the optional **Ensemble configuration** section retained. Cross-linked in both directions with [ECWAM](./ecwam.md).

## What area it covers
- **Coverage:** Global oceans
- **Open Data grid (verified):** regular latitude–longitude, **1440 × 721**, 0.25° × 0.25°, 1,038,240 total grid points
- **Grid origin:** first grid point 90°N / 180°, scanning mode 0 (west→east, north→south). The longitude axis begins at the dateline in the raw GRIB header — see the [IFS entry](../../../nwp_models/global/eu/ifs.md#what-area-it-covers).
- **Sea mask:** GRIB bitmap with **665,628 valid points (64.11%)** — byte-for-byte the same mask as the deterministic ECWAM distribution, static across steps and identical across members.

## Basic details
- **Model type:** Ensemble wave model
- **Grid system:** single regular latitude–longitude grid in the open-data distribution (the operational model runs on the atmospheric reduced Gaussian grid; the published product is interpolated to regular 0.25°)
- **Core wave model:** ECWAM (IFS wave component; third-generation WAM derivative)
- **IFS cycle:** Cycle 50r1 (operational since 12 May 2026)
- **Native horizontal resolution:** **~9 km (TCo1279)** — since Cycle 49r1 the wave grid matches the atmospheric resolution in all forecasts, so ENS-WAM runs at the same native resolution as the deterministic wave forecast and as IFS ENS itself. Before 49r1, ENS-WAM ran at 0.25° against an 18 km and later 9 km atmosphere.
- **Horizontal resolution (Open Data):** 0.25° (~28 km), verified
- **Spectral resolution:** 36 directions × 36 frequencies computed online; archived spectrum carries 36 directions × 29 frequencies. No spectra are distributed in the open subset.
- **Ensemble size:** 51 (1 control + 50 perturbed) — **only the 50 perturbed members are distributed in `waef`**; the control is in the deterministic `wave` stream
- **Forecast length (verified):**
  - **360 h (15 days)** at 00 and 12 UTC
  - **144 h (6 days)** at 06 and 18 UTC
- **Update frequency / cycles:** 4× daily (00, 06, 12, 18 UTC)
- **Temporal output resolution (verified):**
  - 00 / 12 UTC: 3-hourly to +144 h, then 6-hourly +150 h to +360 h — **85 step files**
  - 06 / 18 UTC: 3-hourly to +144 h — **49 step files**
- **Observed publication latency:** whole run published in one batch. Measured 2026-07-30: 00z at 07:40 UTC (**T+7h40m**), 06z at 13:04 UTC (**T+7h04m**), 12z at 19:40 UTC (**T+7h40m**) — identical to the atmospheric `enfo` stream, and consistently ~6 minutes after the deterministic `wave` files of the same cycle.

## Forcing and nesting
- **Wind forcing:** IFS ENS 10 m winds, **two-way coupled** — each wave member is coupled to its corresponding atmospheric member, with the sea state feeding back on the Charnock parameter and hence on surface roughness and air–sea momentum flux within that member.
- **Ice forcing:** IFS ENS sea-ice fields. Cycle 50r1 introduced refined wave–sea-ice coupling alongside the move to the NEMO4-SI3 ocean/sea-ice core.
- **Current forcing:** Active as of Cycle 50r1 — surface currents from the coupled NEMO component influence wave propagation.
- **Nested inside / parent for:** Global parent. Note that the previously asserted ECWAM→ARCWAM boundary relationship is flagged as unverified in the [MET Norway WW3 entry](../../regional/norway/met-norway-ww3.md) and is not restated here as established.

## Ensemble configuration

- **Ensemble size:** **50 perturbed members distributed** (`perturbationNumber` 1–50), plus a control that is **not** in this stream. Post-Cycle-50r1 the wave control forecast lives in the deterministic `wave` stream, mirroring the atmospheric control's move into `oper`. A statistically complete 51-member ensemble requires fetching [ECWAM](./ecwam.md) separately and combining.

- **Source of perturbations:** Inherited from [IFS ENS](../../../ensemble_models/global/eu/ifs-ens.md). The wave model itself is **not** independently perturbed — there is no wave-specific stochastic physics scheme and no perturbation of wave parametrizations. Spread arises from the atmospheric ensemble's initial-condition perturbations (singular vectors + EDA) and its SPP model-uncertainty scheme propagating into the surface wind field, plus, since Cycle 50r1, perturbed sea-ice and current fields from the coupled ocean component. Member *n* of `waef` corresponds to member *n* of `enfo`.

  This matters for interpretation: Cycle 50r1's SPP revision to reduce **excessive 10 m wind spread** in the atmospheric ensemble propagates directly into ENS-WAM. Wave spread characteristics changed at that cycle boundary even though the wave model's own physics changes were unrelated.

- **Resolution / output differences vs deterministic sibling:** **None in the open-data distribution.** Unlike most wave ensembles, ENS-WAM's open subset is *not* coarsened or thinned relative to the deterministic stream — same 0.25° grid, same 1440 × 721 dimensions, same static bitmap, same 85/49 step lists, same 13 parameters, same publication cadence. The differences are in what is *added*: derived probability products in `waef`, versus tropical cyclone tracks in the atmospheric `enfo` (`waef` carries no BUFR products at all).

- **Member packaging:** All 50 members concatenated into a **single GRIB2 file per step** (`...-{step}h-waef-ef.grib2`), one GRIB message per member per parameter — 650 messages per step file (13 parameters × 50 members). No per-member file split. Encoding: `productDefinitionTemplateNumber = 1` (individual ensemble forecast), `mars.stream = waef`, `mars.type = pf`, `typeOfProcessedData = pf`, `tablesVersion = 32`, `grid_ccsds` packing, bitmap present.

  > ⚠️ **`numberOfForecastsInEnsemble` reports 51 in a 50-member file** — the same post-50r1 header inconsistency as the atmospheric ensemble. `typeOfEnsembleForecast` is **255** (missing), so it cannot discriminate control from perturbed either. Size member arrays from the distinct `perturbationNumber` values actually present, not from the declared count.

  > ⚠️ **`generatingProcessIdentifier = 109` does not distinguish the ensemble from the deterministic wave stream** — both use 109. It separates the wave family from the atmospheric streams (161), nothing finer. To tell `wave` from `waef` in a mixed archive, use `mars.stream`, or `mars.type` (`fc` vs `pf`). This is a caveat on the sorting trick noted in the [ECWAM entry](./ecwam.md#notes).

- **Derived products distributed:** Four significant-wave-height exceedance probabilities, and **nothing else**:

  | shortName | Event |
  |---|---|
  | `swhg2` | P(significant wave height > 2 m) |
  | `swhg4` | P(significant wave height > 4 m) |
  | `swhg6` | P(significant wave height > 6 m) |
  | `swhg8` | P(significant wave height > 8 m) |

  Delivered in `...-{240h,360h}-waef-ep.grib2` at **00 and 12 UTC only**, on instantaneous 12-hourly steps: the 240h file covers steps 12–240 (20 steps, 80 records), the 360h file covers 252–360 (10 steps, 40 records). The step in the filename is a range label, not the step of the contained fields.

  > **No ensemble mean or spread is distributed for waves.** This is a real divergence from the atmospheric ensemble, whose `-ep` files are mixed containers carrying `em` and `es` alongside `ep`. The `waef` probability files contain `type=ep` records exclusively — verified on both the 240h and 360h files. Any wave ensemble mean, spread, percentile, or exceedance threshold other than 2/4/6/8 m must be computed from the 50 members directly, which is what makes the 45 GB cycle volume a practical concern rather than an academic one.

## Data assimilation
- **Assimilates wave observations:** Yes — via the IFS analysis that initializes the ensemble; ENS-WAM does not run a separate wave analysis of its own.
- **Observation sources:** Satellite altimeter significant wave height is the primary source; SAR wave spectra are assimilable by the same machinery. **TBD:** the current operational mission list is not stated in the open-data documentation.
- **Method:** Optimum Interpolation (OI) applied to altimeter wave height, following Lionello et al. (1992). Cadence relative to the 12-hour atmospheric assimilation window is **TBD**.

## What it provides

Each of the 50 members carries the **same 13 parameters** as the deterministic wave stream — verified identical by set comparison of the `waef` and `wave` index files.

**Integral sea-state parameters:** `swh` (significant height of combined wind waves and swell), `mwp` (mean wave period), `mp2` (mean zero-crossing wave period), `pp1d` (peak wave period), `mwd` (mean wave direction), `cdww` (coefficient of drag with waves)

**Significant wave height by period band:** `h1012` (10–12 s), `h1214` (12–14 s), `h1417` (14–17 s), `h1721` (17–21 s), `h2125` (21–25 s), `h2530` (25–30 s)

**Static field:** `wmb` (model bathymetry, clipped at 999 m)

Sample decode of `swh` at +24 h from the 00z run: member 1 ranges 0.004–13.665 m (mean 2.069 m), member 50 ranges 0.004–12.319 m (mean 2.066 m) — near-identical means with materially different maxima, the expected signature of an ensemble whose spread concentrates in storm cores.

**Not distributed:** 2D wave spectra, wind-sea / total-swell partition triplets, Stokes drift, wave-induced stress, peak direction, extreme-wave diagnostics, and — as noted above — ensemble mean and spread.

> ⚠️ **`wmb` is duplicated 4,250 times per 00/12 UTC cycle.** The static bathymetry field is emitted for every member at every step (50 × 85), and the values are bit-identical across members and across steps. That is **25.5 MB per step file and 2.17 GB per cycle**, about 4.8% of total volume, carrying zero information beyond the first copy. Fetch it once from any member at any step and skip the other 4,249 via the index offsets.

- **Is the data downloadable?** Yes

  https://data.ecmwf.int/forecasts/

Mirror parity was confirmed on 2026-07-30 between the ECMWF portal and GCS for the `240h-waef-ep.index` object (matching MD5 `4e7acb12dde4...`, 16,552 bytes). **AWS could not be verified in the same session** — the regional endpoint returned HTTP 503 `SlowDown` on every attempt after sustained request volume earlier in the check. Parity is presumed but not established; see the throttling note below.

Full access mechanics — the Azure SAS-token exchange, the GCS bucket identity, and historical path-schema changes — are documented once in the [IFS entry](../../../nwp_models/global/eu/ifs.md#data-availability).

> ⚠️ **AWS throttling is the binding constraint on bulk retrieval from this stream.** Sustained requests return HTTP 503 `SlowDown`. A full 00z ensemble pull is 85 files totalling 45 GB, and per-member extraction via byte ranges multiplies request counts by 50 or 650 depending on granularity. During verification, AWS served requests intermittently and then refused entirely, while GCS and the ECMWF portal handled the same workload without complaint. **Prefer GCS for bulk ensemble work**, and implement exponential backoff regardless of channel. The same applies with more force to the two atmospheric ensembles, which are larger still — see *Volume*.

**Byte-range retrieval is close to mandatory here.** Each step file carries 650 GRIB messages; an individual `swh` member field is ~845 KB against a 529 MB container. Pulling one parameter across all 50 members costs ~42 MB instead of 529 MB — a 12× saving, and far more if only a subset of members or a geographic region is needed. The `.index` sidecar gives `_offset` and `_length` per message; offsets change every run and must be re-read each cycle.

## Notes

- **Verified 2026-07-30.** Stream inventory, step lists, per-member parameter counts, ensemble encoding keys, grid geometry and bitmap, probability-product contents, static-field duplication, volumes, publication latency and partial mirror parity all confirmed by direct inspection of live files from the 00z, 06z and 12z runs and the 2026-07-29 18z run.

- **Documentation discrepancy — the MARS mapping table is stale.** ECMWF's Open Data documentation still maps `waef / cf → waef / ef`, implying the wave control forecast sits inside the ensemble file. It does not. Index inspection at multiple steps returns `type=pf` exclusively, `number` 1–50, with no `cf` record. The table appears not to have been updated for Cycle 50r1.

- **Sea ice is handled by energy suppression, not masking.** The bitmap is static, so seasonally ice-covered water remains inside the valid mask and returns near-zero wave height when ice is present. Roughly 10% of valid sea points carry SWH below 0.05 m in a typical field. This has a specific consequence for ensemble work: **exceedance probabilities and spread statistics computed over the raw mask will include ice-suppressed cells as genuine near-zero forecasts**, biasing basin-wide probability fields. See [ECWAM](./ecwam.md#notes) for the transect evidence.

- **Coastal cells are frequently masked** at 0.25°. The open subset is unsuitable for nearshore probabilistic work without a finer regional model.

- **Fully GRIB2-native encoding.** All 13 parameters decode with `localTablesVersion = 0` (WMO-standard discipline/category/number). ENS-WAM should be unaffected by the Cycle 50r2 parameter-encoding migration, unlike the atmospheric streams.

- **Open subset vs. full operational model.** The free data is a reduced-parameter, 0.25° extract. Full-resolution, full-parameter ensemble wave products including spectra require a Real-time Dissemination service agreement.

- **Family relationships.** Deterministic sibling: [ECWAM](./ecwam.md). Driving atmospheric ensemble: [IFS ENS](../../../ensemble_models/global/eu/ifs-ens.md). Deterministic atmosphere: [IFS](../../../nwp_models/global/eu/ifs.md). Note that coupled ocean-surface fields (`zos`, `sithick`, `sve`, `svn`) are delivered in the *atmospheric* `enfo` stream, not here.

- **Upcoming resolution change.** ECMWF has announced extension of the open subset toward native 9 km later in 2026; a longer-term move to 0.125° to match the ERA6 grid has also been signalled. Neither is live — as of 2026-07-30 only `0p25` exists. Given that this stream is already ~142 GB/day at 0.25°, a 9 km ensemble subset would be a substantial distribution change and is worth watching for whether ECMWF thins the parameter set or step list to compensate.

## Recent version history

### IFS Cycle 50r1 — 12 May 2026 (current)
- **The wave control forecast left this stream.** `stream=waef, type=cf` is deprecated; the control moved to `stream=wave`. Open Data `waef` files carry 50 members instead of 51.
- **Modified SPP configuration** in the driving atmospheric ensemble, reducing excessive 10 m wind spread — this propagates directly into wave spread.
- **New wave interaction with sea ice and ocean currents**; refined wave–sea-ice coupling.
- **Revised wave model bathymetry.**
- No change in horizontal resolution, vertical resolution, or forecast steps for the wave component.

### IFS Cycle 49r1 — 12 November 2024
- **Wave grid revised to match atmospheric resolution in all forecasts** — ENS-WAM moved from 0.25° to ~9 km (TCo1279), eliminating the long-standing resolution gap between the wave ensemble and its driving atmosphere.
- Output spectra reduced from 36 to 29 frequencies to offset the cost; online computation unchanged at 36 × 36.
- SPP replaced SPPT as the atmospheric stochastic physics scheme, changing the character of inherited wave spread.
- New wind-input parametrizations including a gravity–capillary model and non-linear growth rates.

### IFS Cycle 48r1 — 27 June 2023
- Driving atmospheric ensemble resolution increased from 18 km to 9 km, matching HRES.
- GRIB2 output began using CCSDS compression.

### IFS Cycle 41r2 — 8 March 2016
- ENS-WAM resolution increased from 0.5° to 0.25° (days 0–15), against an 18 km atmospheric ensemble.
